SECTION 02The six-tier ladder

Numbered T0–T5 so the six tiers run contiguously. The substance matches the discussion exactly — the "90-day" tier is T4 and the professional tier is T5.

The organising principle: anonymity is permanent and public; accountability is private and earned. An email address is collected so a person can be banned, never so they can be displayed. No handle is ever linked to an identity in any public surface.

Why T4 requires a completed timeline

Tenure alone can be farmed. Requiring that someone has actually been through a visa queue end-to-end ties trust to lived experience, which is the thing this community is uniquely able to verify and the thing a generic forum can never replicate. It also produces the single most valuable feed interaction we can offer: pointing an anxious asker at someone who has finished the exact queue they are sitting in.

Promotion mechanics

Tiers are computed, not granted. A nightly job recalculates trust_tier for every account from the criteria above and writes it to the identity row; promotions are silent and immediate on next request. Demotion happens on upheld reports and is also automatic. Nothing about tier is shown as a score, a badge, or a leaderboard — the only visible tier is T5, and that is a disclosure requirement rather than a reward. This keeps the ladder from becoming a game to farm, consistent with the "cohorts, not leaderboards" principle.

SECTION 03Anti-spam architecture

Read this before setting any T1 number

For anonymous users, per-identity caps are decorative. A device token is free and infinite — clearing localStorage mints a brand-new identity with a fresh counter. So "5 posts and 20 replies per day" is a UX guardrail, not a security control. The only real backstop on T1 is the per-IP-hash cap, which persists across token resets. If we want anonymous writes to be genuinely bounded, the IP cap is the dial that matters, and it must be set deliberately rather than inherited.

That gives us the layered model below. Each layer catches what the one above it misses.

LayerMechanismCatches
1 · Per-IP capsDurable counters keyed on ip_hash. Proposed: 25 posts / 60 replies per IP per day at T1. Signing in lifts the cap, which is the conversion incentive.Token-cycling, casual flooding
2 · Link gatingNo outbound links, phone numbers, or messaging handles below T3. Pattern-matched, not just URL-parsed.Agent touting, scam links — the dominant real-world spam here
3 · Velocity & similaritySame body across 3+ threads, or N writes inside 60s, auto-holds into the existing moderation queue.Automated posting, copy-paste campaigns
4 · Identity costDisposable-domain blocklist at signup. One account per email. Bans key on email hash + device token + IP cluster together.Ban evasion at T2+
5 · Weighted reportsT3 flags count ~3×; T4 flags auto-hide pending review. Feeds the admin queue already shipped.Everything human judgement catches
6 · Shadow-limitingBorderline accounts keep seeing their own content; the feed does not. No signal that a ban occurred.Whack-a-mole re-registration
Blocking defect — fix before anything else ships

The current limiter is an in-process Python dict (_rate_state in community/service.py:56). Every counter resets when the Heroku dyno restarts, and the moment a second web dyno exists each dyno keeps its own tally — so the effective limit silently multiplies by dyno count. Every control in the table above assumes this layer is real. Move it to Postgres (or Redis) first. The code comment already anticipates this; it simply hasn't happened yet.

A real tension worth naming

This audience shares IP addresses more than most — university campuses, share houses, migrant accommodation, and mobile carrier CGNAT all put many legitimate users behind one hash. Aggressive IP caps therefore produce false positives on exactly the people we most want. The resolution is to keep IP caps moderate, make signing in always lift them, and lean on velocity/similarity detection (layer 3) which targets behaviour rather than location. Never hard-ban an IP; throttle it and offer the sign-in path.

Deliberately not doing

  • No SMS verification at launch. It costs money, adds real friction, and for people mid-migration with changing numbers it is actively hostile. Hold it in reserve as an escalation for repeat abusers.
  • No direct messages at launch. Once the feed is policed, touting migrates to DMs where moderation cannot see it. Do not build the channel until there is a reason.
  • No visible karma or leaderboards. Visible points invite farming and contradict the cohort principle.
  • Never gate participation on visa status. No document, no proof, no case number is ever required to take part.

SECTION 04The s276 framing rules

The exposure, stated plainly

Under the Migration Act, giving immigration assistance while unregistered is an offence. The risk to us is not that members share experiences — it is that the product shape makes peer experience look like professional advice. A layout that reads "question in, authoritative answer out" is advice-shaped. A layout that reads "here is what happened to people in your queue" is not. This is an information-architecture decision before it is a legal one, which is why it belongs in the build plan rather than the terms of service.

Rules that bind the interface

RuleImplementation
Never the word "advice"Replies are replies, not answers. The helpful signal marks "this was useful experience", not "this is correct". No "expert", no "verified answer", no "solution".
Composer framingModes are Ask the room and Share your story. Placeholder copy invites what happened to you, not what someone else should do. Persistent line: EXPERIENCES, NOT ADVICE.
No brokered engagementsBelow T5 nobody may offer paid help. Touting patterns (fee offers, WhatsApp/DM solicitation, "I can lodge for you") auto-hold into the queue — this is simultaneously the top spam vector and the top legal vector, so it gets one shared control.
T5 disclosure is mandatoryRegistered agents carry a persistent badge and MARN. Their replies remain general information; anything further moves to a formal engagement off-platform.
Platform voice never instructsWait Check reports where someone sits in a distribution. It never says what to do about it. "One in three people waits longer than you" is a fact; "you should follow up with the department" is advice.
Footer line stays everywhereGeneral information shared between community members — not migration advice. Already live; keep it on every surface including the app shell rail.

SECTION 05Two sources: Official vs Room

Already built — more than expected

The dual-source concept is roughly 70% implemented. VisaReference already carries official_p50_days, official_p90_days and official_updated; processing.py has wait_verdict_official() alongside the community verdict; and the frontend official-times.tsx already computes a delta between community p50 and the official figure. The work here is less "build it" than "make the community half statistically honest, and be clear about where the official half comes from".

The censoring problem — the one thing that must be right

Community percentiles are currently computed from decided cases only. This systematically understates waits, because fast grants leave the queue first while slow cases are still sitting in it — the classic right-censoring bias. If we publish a median of finished cases and call it "the community wait", it will read low, and the people it reads low for are precisely the long-waiting members whose trust we cannot afford to lose.

The engine already has the right instinct: compute_stats() carries a pending count beside the percentiles, and the module docstring calls this "honest denominators". The fix is to finish that thought.

StageApproach
v1 — honestKeep decided-only percentiles, but never display one without its pending count and an explicit label: MEDIAN OF 387 DECIDED · 214 STILL WAITING. Cheap, shippable now, and not misleading.
v2 — correctKaplan–Meier survival estimate using still-waiting cases as censored observations. Produces a median that accounts for people who haven't finished. This is a genuine differentiator: we count the people still waiting; the official number does not.

Cohorting and thresholds

  • Cohort key: subclass → then stream, onshore/offshore, and state where sample size allows. Fall back to the broader cohort rather than publish a thin number.
  • Minimum sample: below n = 20 decided cases, show "not enough data yet" instead of a figure. The current unknown verdict tier already exists for this.
  • Recency window: lodgements within the last 12 months. Policy changes shift queues, and a five-year median describes a queue that no longer exists.
  • Sample isolation: scraped seed timelines are flagged is_sample and excluded from stats today. See the open decision in §10 — with them excluded, most cohorts have no publishable number at launch.

The official half

Official figures live in VisaReference and are seeded by hand — official_updated is a human label like "Mar 2026". The mockup's earlier "checked daily" claim was wrong and has been corrected. Either automate ingestion from the Home Affairs published tool, or keep it manual and always display the as-at date. Manual with an honest date is perfectly acceptable at this stage; a stale figure presented as live is not.

Migration 3 — professional verification

New professional_verifications: user_id, marn, legal_name, register_checked_at, checked_by, status, expires_on. Deliberately separate from users so real-name data lives in one auditable place and never leaks into community responses.

Privacy rule — cheap now, painful later

If a community member later becomes a client of a consultant on the SaaS side, that consultant must never be able to discover their community handle. Same users row, but handle must be excluded from every consultant-facing serializer by default. Write this as a test, not a convention — the failure mode is a migrant discovering their agent can read their anonymous posts about that agent.

SECTION 10Open decisions

Four things this plan cannot settle on its own.

1 · Do the 141 scraped timelines feed the public numbers?

They are flagged is_sample and excluded from stats today. Excluded, most cohorts have no publishable community median at launch — the dual-source table would show "Official" beside a dash. Included, the numbers work immediately but rest partly on forum-scraped data rather than first-party reports.

RECOMMENDATION · Include them, but label the provenance in the open — "based on 141 reported timelines, 66 collected from public forums". Transparency about where a number comes from is more defensible than a dash, and it is on-brand.

2 · What is the real per-IP cap for anonymous writes?

The agreed 5 posts / 20 replies is per identity, which anonymous users can reset at will. The IP cap is the control that actually binds. Set it too low and share houses and campuses get blocked; too high and the T1 tier is effectively uncapped.

RECOMMENDATION · Start at 25 posts / 60 replies per IP per day, log every rejection, and tune from the first fortnight of real traffic rather than guessing now.

3 · Is Wait Check usable without an account?

It is the strongest acquisition hook and the most shareable artefact, which argues for leaving it fully open. But it is also the natural moment to ask someone to contribute their own timeline.

RECOMMENDATION · Fully open, no account, no gate. Ask for the timeline after showing the result, when the value is already delivered.

4 · Does an anonymous member keep their handle when they sign up?

Keeping it preserves continuity and the small reputation they've built. Rotating it on signup severs any link between pre- and post-account activity, which is marginally better for privacy.

RECOMMENDATION · Keep the handle, and say so at the gate. Losing your identity is a reason not to sign up, and the privacy gain is theoretical since the account was already pseudonymous.
